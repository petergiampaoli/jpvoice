The apartment was on the fourth floor and there was no lift. She carried the box up and put it down outside the door. The nameplate had been taken off. There were two holes in the wood where the screws had been.

She had not been here for eleven years. The corridor smelled of cooking, the same way it had then. Somewhere a television was on.

She found the key in her coat pocket. For a while she did not put it in the lock.

The room was small. There was a table, a chair, a cupboard, and a bed against the wall. The bed had been made. That was the first thing she noticed — that the bed had been made, and that the sheets were clean.

On the table there was a cup with a chip at the rim. Beside it, a receipt from the shop on the corner, dated the third of March. She looked at the receipt for a while. Then she put it back exactly where it had been.

In the cupboard there were three coats. One of them was a red coat she remembered. It was too small for her now, or she had been smaller then. She did not take it out.

In any case, she thought, it did not matter. She sat down on the chair and put her hands on her knees. The window faced the wall of the next building. There was a drainpipe, and a window, and beyond that a grey strip of sky.

She said, aloud, that she would stay for two days. Then she said nothing.
